# low_vol (2026-11-19)

**Verdict: FAIL** (trial #2)

## Hypothesis
Investors overpay for volatile/lottery-like stocks and leverage-constrained investors tilt to high beta, so low-risk names earn higher risk-adjusted returns. Score = minus the rolling std of daily returns; higher = buy.

## Source
NSE low-risk anomaly study Jan 2001-Jun 2016 (abstract seen via search 2026-11-19); Agarwalla et al. 2014 BAB India (seen cited). Journal articles; effect size sample-dependent. Seed idea was written from US-centred memory; India evidence exists but is mixed (see `research/india_evidence.md`, 2026-11-19 round; no new search needed).

## Checks before testing (no backtest results used)

## What we learned
Cost is not the reason: charges were 0.35%/yr and results did not improve at 25,000 (0.45%) or 100,000 (1.8%). Exposure was only 25-35% in most OOS years, so the 200 SMA / market-regime filter and bear exit kept the sleeve largely in cash: an exposure effect, since low-vol names are often below their 200 SMA during rallies led by high-beta stocks. Low_vol also fell below the random-pick control. In this universe and OOS window (2021-2026) the effect did not show up under these rotation rules. Per the rules, no re-tuning in this session; any variant (e.g. no bear exit) is a new trial. Backtest has survivorship bias; not evidence either way.
