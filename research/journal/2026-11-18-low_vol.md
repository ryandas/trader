# 2026-11-18: low_vol (trial #1) - FAIL

- **Hypothesis**: low trailing-volatility stocks earn equal or better risk-adjusted returns than volatile ones.
- **Source**: Baker, Bradley & Wurgler (2011); Ang et al. (2006); NSE Nifty100 Low Volatility 30 methodology (verify citations).
- **Grid** (fixed before any result): window 252 and window 126 daily returns; top 10, buffer 10, bear exit, monthly.
- **Setup**: liquid top-100 universe, INR 5,000, real costs, walk-forward 3y IS / 1y OOS, OOS 2021-01-01 to 2026-11-17.

| gate | result |
|---|---|
| no_lookahead | pass |
| beats index and basket | FAIL: -0.98% vs index 12.9% vs equal-weight basket 16.3% |
| beats index in most years | FAIL: 1 of 6 |
| beats random picks | FAIL: -0.98% vs random median 4.0% |
| robust across grid | FAIL: both points below the index (-1.0%, +0.8%) |
| deflated Sharpe | FAIL: 0.153 after 1 counted trial |
| charges < 3% | pass: 1.16% |
| not a duplicate | pass: correlation 0.28 with default momentum |

Full universe (reported only): 0.95% CAGR. Max drawdown -8.4%, 21 trades in 6 years.

## What happened
Exposure was only 0.3-14% by year. With 10 slots of about INR 500, a stock priced above INR 500 cannot be bought
(`floor(500 / price) = 0`), and low-volatility names in the liquid top 100 are mostly expensive large caps. So the
book was mostly cash and the result mixes "low volatility" with "can I afford one share". That was my first reading,
and it was incomplete: the account-size recheck (not counted as a trial) shows the idea also fails with plenty of
capital.

| account | candidate CAGR | index | basket | years beating index |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5,000 | -1.0% | 12.9% | 16.3% | 1/6 |
| 25,000 | 2.3% | 12.9% | 16.3% | 0/6 |
| 100,000 | 3.9% | 12.9% | 16.3% | 0/6 |

So affordability explains the idle cash at INR 5,000, but low volatility genuinely lags on this universe even at
INR 100,000 (merit: no).

## Follow-up
The affordability-aware variant is no longer worth a trial slot: the idea fails at 100,000 too.
